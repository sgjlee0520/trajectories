Source: India Today archives; HCL corporate history (https://www.hcltech.com/about-us/history-and-heritage)

**Upper end:** ≥₹15 Crore (~$12.12M) in ~1985, vs the bar of $3,342,206 for 1985 — above.

The evidence for 1985 comes from a regional sales figure. An India Today article from October 15, 1986 about HCL's price war reports that one sales region alone (Neelam Dhawan's territory) had company revenue of ₹15 Crore at the start of the period and ₹25 Crore by 1986. Since this is a *single sales region*, total company revenue must be ≥₹15 Crore. The 1985 threshold in INR is only ₹4.14 Crore (= $3,342,206 at 12.38 INR/USD), so even a single region's revenue exceeds the whole-company threshold.

> "Her performance during that period—which reportedly included a 20-fold jump in personal computer sales—saw company revenue for her region grow from Rs 15 crore to Rs 25 crore."

Source: India Today, October 15, 1986 — "HCL slashes computer prices, sparks off fresh price war" (https://www.indiatoday.in/magazine/business/story/19861015-hcl-slashes-computer-prices-sparks-off-fresh-price-war-801332-1986-10-15)

**Supporting evidence (1987):** ₹100 Crore group turnover (~$77.16M) in October 1987, vs interpolated bar of ~$3,629,213 — above.
> "HCL, which is a Rs 100-crore group today and far bigger than DCM's computer division..."

Source: India Today, October 31, 1987 — "Entrepreneurship: New Indian millionaires and how they made it" (https://www.indiatoday.in/magazine/special-report/story/19871031-entrepreneurship-new-indian-millionaires-and-how-they-made-it-799446-1987-10-31)

**Currency conversion:** INR/USD rates from RBI annual averages:
- 1976: 8.96 INR/USD
- 1985: 12.38 INR/USD
- 1986: 12.61 INR/USD
- 1987: 12.96 INR/USD

**What I could not find:** Exact total company revenue figures for any year between 1977 and 1986. The ₹15 Crore figure is a single *region's* revenue, not the whole company. No audited annual revenue figures from HCL's pre-1990 era appear to be publicly available online.

**Doubts:** The ₹15 Crore regional figure is the strongest evidence but is indirect — it proves total company revenue ≥₹15 Crore, but doesn't give the exact total. The exact year of the ₹15 Crore figure is uncertain (could be 1984 or 1985). However, note how low the bar is: the 1985 threshold is only ₹4.14 Crore (~$334K in today's terms), so even a modest-sized Indian IT company in 1985 would clear it. The real crossing likely occurred in 1982–1984, but without sourced revenue data I cannot pin it down. If the ₹15 Crore regional figure is rejected (because it's regional, not total), the next clearly sourced total company figure is ₹100 Crore in 1987, which would give a range of 1977–1987 (11 years) — exceeding the 10-year limit and making the answer `unknown`.
