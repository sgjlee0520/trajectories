## p33 — Toni Morrison (First book to reach 1,000,000+ cumulative sales)

**Answer: `1996–1998`** — Song of Solomon (1977)

### The crossing

**Song of Solomon** was selected for **Oprah's Book Club in December 1996** (it was the second-ever selection). Following this, sales surged dramatically.

In a **January 19, 1998** TIME Magazine cover story titled "Paradise Found" (by Paul Gray), Toni Morrison herself confirmed the milestone:

> *"A million copies of that book sold," she says, again shaking her head. "And sales of my other books in paperback jumped about 25%."*

- Source: TIME Magazine, January 19, 1998, "Paradise Found"
- URL: https://time.com/vault/issue/1998-01-19/page/68/

### Pre-Oprah baseline

Before the Oprah selection, Song of Solomon had approximately 570,000 copies in print by 1979 (paperback edition). Sales slowed significantly — ~14,000 copies in 1992, rising to ~98,500 in 1993 after the Nobel Prize. The book did NOT reach 1 million copies before the Oprah selection.

### Why 1996–1998 and not a single year

The Oprah selection was December 1996. The million-copy milestone was confirmed by Morrison in January 1998. The actual crossing likely occurred in 1997, but the bounded range based on sourced data is **1996 (Oprah selection triggered the surge) to 1998 (Morrison confirmed the million)**.

### Other books

- **Beloved (1987):** Eventually sold 10M+ worldwide, but no documented evidence it hit 1M before the 1996 Oprah effect. Massive numbers came from school curricula and the 1998 film.
- **The Bluest Eye (1970):** Selected by Oprah in 2000, adding ~800K copies.
- **Paradise (1998):** Sold 1M within weeks of publication — but this was after Song of Solomon had already achieved the milestone.

### Adjudication

The second researcher's position (2019 as the answer) is **too conservative and dates the crossing too late**, which the task identifies as "the single worst error available here." The Forbes estimate is journalistic, but it is:
1. From a reputable outlet (Forbes)
2. Written by a named, credible journalist (Matthew Herper)
3. Explicitly distinguished from funding
4. Consistent with the trajectory ($20M+ in 2017 → $54M in 2019 → $77M in 2020)

The first researcher's range of **2008–2017** is valid:
- Lower bound: 2008 (founding year — company cannot earn before it exists)
- Upper bound: 2017 (Forbes revenue estimate above bar)

The Forbes figure is a forward-looking journalist's estimate ("will log"), not an audited figure. However, rejecting it entirely would force us to use 2019, which the task explicitly warns against: waiting for the first year a company "happened to disclose revenue" dates the career too late. The Forbes figure is sufficiently credible to serve as the upper bound of a range.
